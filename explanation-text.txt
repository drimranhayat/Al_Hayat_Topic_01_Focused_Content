Relieving distress|The first promise links relieving a believer's worldly hardship with relief from a hardship of the Day of Resurrection. Al-Nawawi explains removing distress broadly through useful knowledge, money, practical assistance, advice or other available benefit. The concern is concrete help, not only sympathetic words.|p,c

Al-Nawawi highlights granting time to a debtor in hardship and the virtue of covering faults, while referring the latter's detailed limits to earlier discussion. Helping others and receiving help|The following sentence states that Allah aids the servant while the servant aids a brother. It binds the spiritual promise to an outward act of service. ##66272
The request and the answer|The Prophet is asked to invoke a curse against the polytheists. He answers by describing his mission: he was not sent to be a habitual invoker of curses, but as a mercy. The report's subject is his response to that request, not an account of a curse he went on to pronounce.|p
What the commentary explains about cursing|Qadi Iyad discusses the report alongside other warnings against cursing. He explains that the term has come to mean a prayer for exclusion from Allah's mercy, and contrasts habitual cursing with the mercy, cooperation and mutual care described as the character of believers. That surrounding discussion explains why the Prophet's answer is morally significant.|c
The defining direction of the mission|The conclusion directs attention from the requested condemnation to mercy as the character of the mission. It should retain that clear emphasis without being expanded into a claim that this short narration resolves every separate legal discussion of supplication, conflict or particular historical incidents.|p,c
